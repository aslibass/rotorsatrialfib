# Patient Management Test Scenarios

Use this file for scenario-based clinical testing.

## Scenario 1
- Title: 72M with worsening intermittent chest pain and dyspnea
- Clinical context: Acute cardiology assessment for possible ischemia and decompensated heart failure in a high-risk older adult.
- Patient profile:
  - Age/Sex: 72-year-old male
  - Key comorbidities: Diabetes mellitus, hypertension, possible known CAD, ex-smoker (quit 5 years ago)
  - Current medications: Metformin, insulin, ACE inhibitor, calcium channel blocker, aspirin, statin, beta blocker
- Presenting problem: Intermittent chest pain and shortness of breath, worsening over the past few days.
- Relevant findings (labs/imaging/ECG):
  - Social context: Lives alone, wife deceased
  - Examination: BP 150/90, ejection systolic murmur in aortic area, diminished peripheral pulses with delayed upstroke, elevated JVP, bibasal crackles with reduced lower-zone breath sounds
  - ECG: Sinus rhythm, 88 bpm, lateral ST depression and inferior T-wave inversion, no ST elevation
  - Troponin (reported): 0.08 ng/L (reported local reference normal <4.0 ng/L)
  - CXR: Mild cardiomegaly and pulmonary congestion
- Management question: What is the immediate management plan in the first 24 hours, including differential prioritization (ACS vs acute decompensated HF vs significant aortic stenosis physiology), investigations to confirm etiology, and safe escalation thresholds?
- Expected evidence/guideline anchors:
  - Acute chest pain risk stratification pathways
  - NSTE-ACS evaluation principles (serial troponin and dynamic ECG strategy)
  - Acute heart failure/congestion management principles
  - Valvular heart disease (suspected severe AS) considerations when selecting vasodilators/afterload-reducing agents
  - Secondary prevention optimization in CAD/DM/HTN
- Success criteria for answer:
  - Provides a time-ordered plan (immediate, 0-6h, 6-24h)
  - Includes monitoring, repeat ECG/troponin strategy, and echo urgency
  - Balances ischemia treatment with hemodynamic/valvular risk
  - Defines disposition (CCU/monitored bed) and triggers for urgent invasive evaluation
  - Cites relevant guideline families (ACC/ESC) and clearly explains rationale
